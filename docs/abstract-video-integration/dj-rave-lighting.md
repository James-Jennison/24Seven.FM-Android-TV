# DJ Soff Snep native rave lighting

The original booth, rotating platter markings, LCD animation, panel indicators and Soff Snep artwork are retained. The offline video no longer animates venue lighting. A native hardware-Canvas layer now renders 28 coordinated moving-head beams, four laser fans, drifting haze, blue/purple/magenta/cyan washes and spatial white backlight accents from current decoded PCM features.

`RaveLightingDrive` consumes existing calibrated amplitude/frequency levels, the 32-bin spectrum and transient envelope. Distinct measurement timestamps feed adaptive onset detection, refractory kick events, confidence-gated tempo estimation, energy baselines and quiet/build/drop inference. Mid-band colour decisions land on four-event boundaries; movement varies with mids and build/drop energy, while highs control fine laser accents. White bursts require measured kicks and a 400 ms minimum interval. Reduced intensity removes white bursts and reduces brightness, fixture/ray counts and movement. Missing, silent or >500 ms stale PCM clears the lighting; entry/resume does not invent a beat or drop. Tempo/drop recognition is heuristic, not a ground-truth music annotation.

The view adds no audio recorder, player, MediaSession, network connection, autonomous animation callback or focus target. The existing lifecycle-bound presentation tick drives both video and lighting. The scene transform is shared. A bounded offscreen light layer uses soft foreground attenuation and a luminance-derived mask from the original logo bitmap; hard rectangular logo/foreground clipping was rejected after physical visual inspection. Cached gradients and reusable drawing objects bound per-frame allocations. The light view uses a one-third-resolution hardware layer scaled to the exact parent dimensions; inverse scale before the shared video transform keeps the logo/foreground mask aligned, including non-divisible dimensions. The photographic video stays full resolution. A full-resolution light layer was rejected after measured render latency; a software raster experiment was also rejected after substantially worse CPU timings.

Android rendering reference: [hardware acceleration](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/hardware-accel) and [Canvas API](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/graphics/Canvas.html).

Validation: controller tests cover measured 120 BPM onset trains, duplicate samples, quiet/drop and build transitions, frequency-specific responses, mid-band colour choices, stale/future/invalid measurements and reduced intensity. Physical testing covers actual compositing through a controlled musical fixture, continuous live station PCM, decoder loops, silence, recreation and style switching. Corrected physical images pass the seam regression (6.98 <20), 112 unit tests and all three physical tests pass, and the decoder completes the loop with 705 buffers and zero drops. Final independent review: Claude, Gemini and DeepSeek completed with no findings; closure-e04f21df78b7 is READY (change-f7de32707fe0).
